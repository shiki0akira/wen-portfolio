## Overview

Woohop is an icebreaker toolkit for anyone leading a group. No props, no rules to memorise: pick a game the night before, then follow your phone step by step on the day.

It started as Web100, a series of party games you play right in the browser. After six games launched and found users, I redesigned it into Woohop. From planning and design to development and launch, everything was built with AI.

## Version 1: many small games first

The original problem was simple:

- **Hosting takes prep**: for parties and icebreakers, hosts have to look up rules, memorise scripts and prepare props.
- **Existing tools ask too much**: many games require an app download or an account, and nobody wants to wait around.

So the first version was a set of standalone games. Each one launches on its own, can be found in search on its own, and quickly shows whether people actually play it. The first one took just 4 evenings.

| Product | How it plays |
|---|---|
| This or That | Answer a few questions, get grouped and see what you have in common |
| Happiness Bingo | Bingo where you find people, not numbers |
| Time Bomb | Pass one phone around; whoever holds it when it goes off loses |
| Buzzer quiz | Scan a QR code to join and race to answer |
| Avalon voice host | Reads the host's lines aloud automatically |
| Happiness Simulator | A big screen plus everyone's phone ([read case](/en/work/happiness/)) |

## Why redesign

As the number of games grew, the problem shifted from "there's no tool" to "which one do I pick?"

The people who actually open the site are usually **leaders running an event tomorrow**. They don't care about a list of games. They want to know:

- How many of us are there? Does everyone have a phone? Which game fits?
- One game isn't enough. How do I order a warm-up and a get-to-know-you?
- It's my first time leading. What if it falls flat?

The old home page was just a row of games, leaving all of these questions to the user. So the redesign turned **a game collection into a toolkit for leaders**, and the name changed from Web100 to Woohop: easier to remember and more like a party.

## What changed

### 1. Help me pick: start with the group

The home page asks just two questions: how many people, and whether they have phones. Matching games appear right away, each card labelled with its purpose (warm-up or get to know each other), group size, length and phone needs. Anyone who doesn't want to choose can hit "Surprise me".

![Help me pick: choose group size and phones to see matching games](/images/web100/web100-finder-en.webp)

### 2. Icebreaker combo: a ready-made flow

An icebreaker is only the start of a gathering. Choose 10, 15 or 20 minutes and Woohop pairs a warm-up with a get-to-know-you game and adds up the total time, so you can move straight into the main event.

![Icebreaker combo: a warm-up and a get-to-know-you game sized to your time](/images/web100/web100-combo-en.webp)

### 3. Organised by situation, not by game type

The Avalon voice host lives under "Board games", and Happiness Simulator under its own small-group section. Leaders come in through their own situation and find the right tool fast.

### 4. Next up: party room and guides

- **Party room**: everyone joins once. When the host picks a game, every phone switches over together and returns to the lobby afterwards, with no rescanning for each game.
- **Guides**: a sample 15-minute flow for first-time leaders and lines to rescue a quiet room, so people without experience feel ready to lead.

![Party room: open once, play all night](/images/web100/web100-party-en.webp)

## Key decisions

### 1. Validate with small products, then combine them

Building a big platform first makes it hard to know what people want. Shipping small games first showed that "This or That" was the favourite and that leaders were the main users, and the redesign was built on those findings.

### 2. Only two filters up front

Group size and phones are the two things people can answer fastest on the spot, and they decide the most. Other filters live on the Find games page so the home page stays simple.

### 3. Fewer settings, faster starts

Play-testing with friends and groups showed that people had fun, but too many settings before starting killed the mood. Settings were cut to the minimum so a game starts almost as soon as it opens. The party room follows the same idea: joining happens only once.

### 4. Eight languages from day one

Icebreakers are needed everywhere. With AI, translation became cheap, so every new feature in the redesign also ships in 8 languages.

## Most popular: This or That

Everyone answers a few either-or questions, like "cats or dogs". The game then groups people with similar tastes and lists what they have in common, so people get to know each other quickly and find someone to talk to.
